ShopRank term cards: weeks 1-3

Click a card to open it: definition, what it's for, a real-life example, and where it's used. Mark each one "I know it" or "Review". Your marks are saved in this browser only.

QueryWeek 1

The text a shopper types into a search box.

Used for: It is the input to every search system.

Real life: At Lulu you ask the salesman "55 inch black Samsung TV".

Where it's used: ShopRank: 97,345 US queries. Every search engine (Amazon, Flipkart, Google).

CandidateWeek 1

A product already picked as possibly relevant for a query, before final ordering.

Used for: Keeps the expensive ranker small: rank 20 items, not 1.2M.

Real life: The salesman brings 20 TVs from the back store to the counter.

Where it's used: ESCI Task 1 gives ~20 candidates per query. Industry: retrieval returns 100-1000 candidates.

Retrieval vs re-rankingWeek 1

Retrieval = fast, rough search over millions. Re-ranking = slower, careful ordering of the few found.

Used for: Two stages: cheap and wide first, accurate and narrow second.

Real life: A recruiter skims 1,000 CVs to get 20, then interviews the 20 carefully.

Where it's used: ShopRank is a re-ranker (run_task1.py). Industry: BM25/ANN retrieval, then LambdaMART or a cross-encoder.

ESCI labelsWeek 1

Exact, Substitute, Complement, Irrelevant: how well a product matches a query.

Used for: Graded relevance: more detailed than right or wrong.

Real life: Ask for a Samsung 55" TV: Samsung 55" = Exact, LG 55" = Substitute, remote = Complement, phone case = Irrelevant.

Where it's used: Amazon Shopping Queries dataset, 1.82M US labels (69% E, 20% S, 9% I, 2% C).

GainWeek 1

The number given to each label: E 1, S 0.1, C 0.01, I 0.

Used for: Turns labels into points so a ranking can be scored.

Real life: Exam marks: a full answer 10, a partly right one 1, an off-topic one 0.

Where it's used: ingest.py CASTs labels to DOUBLE gains. Must match LightGBM label_gain.

Train / dev / test splitWeek 1

Train = learn from it. Dev = tune and compare. Test = final, one-time report.

Used for: Measures how the model does on data it has never seen.

Real life: Practise on old papers (train), take mock exams (dev), sit the real exam once (test).

Where it's used: ShopRank: dev = 1 in 10 train queries by MD5 hash; test locked behind --allow-test.

Data leakageWeek 1

Information from the answer or the test data sneaking into training.

Used for: Leakage makes scores look better than reality.

Real life: Seeing the exam questions before the exam: a high mark, but you learned nothing.

Where it's used: ShopRank splits by query; the test test_no_query_in_two_splits enforces it. A feature built from labels would leak.

DCGWeek 1

Discounted cumulative gain: add each item's gain divided by log2(position+1).

Used for: Rewards relevant items near the top more than lower down.

Real life: A shop's first shelf at eye level sells most; lower shelves sell less.

Where it's used: metrics.py dcg_at_k. Used in TREC and industry search evaluation.

nDCGWeek 1

DCG divided by the best possible DCG, so it is between 0 and 1.

Used for: Compares rankings across queries fairly.

Real life: Your marks divided by the maximum marks = a percentage.

Where it's used: Official ESCI Task 1 metric (trec_eval, full list). ShopRank best: 0.836 on dev.

nDCG@10Week 1

nDCG counting only the top 10 results.

Used for: Matches what a user sees on page 1.

Real life: Only the first page of Google results matters to most people.

Where it's used: ShopRank reports both @10 and full list; full list = the official metric.

MRRWeek 1

Mean reciprocal rank: the average of 1 / position of the first relevant item.

Used for: Asks: how fast does the first good answer appear?

Real life: First good answer at position 1 = 1.0, at position 4 = 0.25.

Where it's used: ShopRank reports MRR (Exact). Jruva also uses MRR for retrieval.

Recall@kWeek 1

Share of all relevant items that appear in the top k.

Used for: Checks that nothing important was missed.

Real life: You have 5 good shirts in the shop; 3 appear on the first rack, so recall = 0.6.

Where it's used: Used for retrieval stages, where missing items is the main risk.

Paired bootstrap / 95% CIWeek 1

Resample queries 10,000 times to see the range a difference could take.

Used for: Tells you whether an improvement is real or luck.

Real life: Taste 10,000 random spoonfuls of two curries to be sure one is really saltier.

Where it's used: bootstrap.py. If the interval stays above 0, the gain is real. Used in A/B testing.

BM25Week 1

A keyword scoring formula: rare words count more, repeats saturate, long text is penalised.

Used for: A strong, cheap baseline for text search.

Real life: A librarian who trusts rare words ("Kindle") more than common ones ("for").

Where it's used: bm25.py; ShopRank title BM25 = 0.819. Default scoring in Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Lucene.

IDFWeek 1

Inverse document frequency: log of how rare a word is across products.

Used for: Makes informative words matter more.

Real life: "Biryani" on a menu tells you more than "rice".

Where it's used: BM25 formula: ln(1 + (N - n + 0.5)/(n + 0.5)).

k1 and bWeek 1

k1 = how fast repeated words stop adding score. b = how much long text is penalised.

Used for: The tuning knobs of BM25.

Real life: Saying "cheap" 10 times in an ad shouldn't make it 10x more relevant (k1).

Where it's used: ShopRank: k1 1.5, b 0.75. b = 0 was clearly worse (-0.0105).

BaselineWeek 1

The simple method every new model must beat.

Used for: Shows whether the complex work is worth it.

Real life: A new cricket bowler must beat the team's current bowler to get picked.

Where it's used: ShopRank baselines: random 0.750, BM25 0.819.

Label noiseWeek 1

Wrong human labels in the data.

Used for: Caps the best possible score; don't overfit to it.

Real life: An answer key with a few wrong answers.

Where it's used: ESCI: "iqos heat not burn" has Duracell batteries labelled Exact.

FeatureWeek 2

One number describing a (query, product) pair.

Used for: Inputs that a model learns from.

Real life: Judging a TV: screen size match, brand match, price; each is a feature.

Where it's used: ShopRank: 14 features in features/build.py.

Feature engineeringWeek 2

Turning domain knowledge into numbers.

Used for: Gives the model useful clues.

Real life: A shopkeeper knows size matters, so you add "is 55 in the title?".

Where it's used: numbers_missing was the 4th most useful feature.

title_coverageWeek 2

Share of query words found in the product title.

Used for: The strongest ShopRank feature.

Real life: All 4 words of "samsung tv 55 black" in the title = 1.0.

Where it's used: Top LightGBM importance in week 2.

Jaccard similarityWeek 2

Shared words divided by all distinct words in both texts.

Used for: Measures word overlap fairly.

Real life: Two shopping lists with 4 items in common out of 6 different items = 0.67.

Where it's used: Feature title_jaccard.

Logistic regressionWeek 2

A model that adds weighted features, then squashes the sum to a 0-1 probability.

Used for: A simple, explainable classifier.

Real life: A bank scoring a loan: income +, debt -, gives the chance of repayment.

Where it's used: ShopRank: written from scratch in NumPy, 0.8296, same as scikit-learn.

SigmoidWeek 2

1 / (1 + e^-z): turns any number into 0-1.

Used for: Converts a score into a probability.

Real life: A dimmer switch: from fully off to fully on, smoothly.

Where it's used: Inside logistic regression; also used in neural networks.

Log lossWeek 2

Measures how wrong a probability is; confident wrong answers cost a lot.

Used for: The thing training makes small.

Real life: Saying "100% sure" and being wrong hurts your reputation more than saying "60% sure".

Where it's used: Logistic regression training objective.

Gradient descentWeek 2

Repeatedly change the weights a little in the direction that lowers the loss.

Used for: How almost every ML model learns.

Real life: Walking downhill in fog: feel the slope, take a small step, repeat.

Where it's used: Week 2 logistic regression; week 3 neural networks; all deep learning.

Learning rateWeek 2

How big each gradient descent step is.

Used for: Too big overshoots; too small is slow.

Real life: Steps down a hill: giant jumps miss the valley, tiny steps take all day.

Where it's used: LambdaMART learning_rate 0.05; AdamW 1e-3 in week 3.

StandardisationWeek 2

Rescale each feature to mean 0, standard deviation 1, using train data.

Used for: Stops big-number features from dominating.

Real life: Comparing marks out of 100 and out of 10 by converting both to percentages.

Where it's used: Use train mean/std on dev/test, never their own (leakage).

L2 regularisationWeek 2

A penalty on big weights.

Used for: Reduces overfitting.

Real life: A teacher who marks down answers relying on one memorised fact.

Where it's used: Logistic regression; weight decay in AdamW is similar.

OverfittingWeek 2

Memorising training data, so new data does worse.

Used for: The main failure to guard against.

Real life: A student who memorises last year's paper and fails a new one.

Where it's used: Guarded by dev split, early stopping, regularisation.

Pointwise / pairwise / listwiseWeek 2

Score each item alone / compare pairs / optimise the whole list's metric.

Used for: Three families of learning to rank.

Real life: Grade each student alone / compare two at a time / decide the class ranking.

Where it's used: ShopRank: logistic regression is pointwise; LambdaMART pairwise/listwise, and it won by +0.0062.

Decision treeWeek 2

A model that asks yes/no questions on features to reach a score.

Used for: Captures if-then patterns.

Real life: "Is coverage > 0.8? Is the brand in the query? Then score +0.3."

Where it's used: The building block of LambdaMART.

Gradient boostingWeek 2

Build trees one after another, each fixing the mistakes of the ones before.

Used for: The strongest method for tabular data.

Real life: Each new tutor works on the topics the student still gets wrong.

Where it's used: LightGBM, XGBoost. ShopRank used 274 trees.

LambdaMARTWeek 2

Gradient-boosted trees trained with lambdas: push hardest on swaps that change nDCG most.

Used for: Learning to rank that optimises the ranking metric.

Real life: A coach who fixes mistakes that cost the most points first.

Where it's used: ShopRank best: 0.8359. Came from Microsoft Research (Burges); used in web search.

Early stoppingWeek 2

Stop adding trees when a held-out slice stops improving.

Used for: Prevents overfitting and wasted time.

Real life: Stop revising a topic when mock-test marks stop going up.

Where it's used: ShopRank held out 10% of TRAIN queries, not dev.

Feature importanceWeek 2

How much each feature helped the model's decisions.

Used for: Explains the model; finds useful clues.

Real life: Which ingredient changes the taste of a dish most.

Where it's used: LightGBM gain: title_coverage, bm25_title, title_jaccard, numbers_missing.

LightGBMWeek 2

Microsoft's fast gradient-boosting library.

Used for: Trains LambdaMART on large tables quickly.

Real life: A fast industrial machine for the boosting recipe.

Where it's used: ShopRank week 2; label_gain set to ESCI gains.

Neural network (MLP)Week 3

Layers of weighted sums with a non-linearity between them.

Used for: Learns complex patterns from data.

Real life: A team where each person passes a refined opinion to the next.

Where it's used: scratch/mlp.py; base of every deep learning model.

ReLUWeek 3

max(0, x): pass positive values, block negatives.

Used for: Adds non-linearity so layers can learn curves.

Real life: A gate that opens only for positive signals.

Where it's used: Used in ShopRank's towers and most modern networks.

AutogradWeek 3

Automatically computes the gradient of the loss for every weight.

Used for: Makes training big networks possible.

Real life: Every number keeps a receipt of how it was made, then you trace back.

Where it's used: scratch/autograd.py matches PyTorch to 9 decimals; PyTorch loss.backward().

Chain ruleWeek 3

Gradient through a chain of steps = product of each step's local slope.

Used for: The maths behind backpropagation.

Real life: A price change passing through supplier, shop, then customer.

Where it's used: Every backward pass in PyTorch.

EmbeddingWeek 3

A list of numbers representing text, so similar meanings are close.

Used for: Lets a model compare meaning, not just words.

Real life: Placing cities on a map: Kochi is near Kozhikode, far from Delhi.

Where it's used: ShopRank two-tower: 128 numbers. Jruva: BGE-small, 384 numbers.

Cosine similarityWeek 3

How close two vectors point in the same direction, from -1 to 1.

Used for: Scores query-product similarity.

Real life: Two arrows pointing the same way = 1, opposite = -1.

Where it's used: Two-tower scoring; vector databases like Qdrant.

Two-tower modelWeek 3

One network encodes queries, another encodes products; compare by cosine.

Used for: Products are encoded once ahead of time, so search is fast at scale.

Real life: Pre-printing every product's ID card; at search time only the query needs a card.

Where it's used: ShopRank week 3: 0.785. Used for large-scale retrieval and recommendations (e.g. YouTube).

Hashing trickWeek 3

Map text pieces to a fixed number of slots with a hash, no vocabulary file.

Used for: Handles new and misspelled words.

Real life: Sorting letters into 262,144 pigeonholes by a fixed rule.

Where it's used: dense/hashing.py: 262,144 buckets.

Character trigramsWeek 3

3-letter pieces of words, e.g. "ann", "nna".

Used for: Makes misspellings share pieces with the right word.

Real life: "anna" and "ana" share "#an" and "na#".

Where it's used: ShopRank hashing; robust to typos.

SoftmaxWeek 3

Turns a list of scores into probabilities that add to 1.

Used for: Picks the most likely option smoothly.

Real life: Splitting 100 votes among candidates by their popularity.

Where it's used: Inside InfoNCE and every classifier.

InfoNCE lossWeek 3

Pick the right product out of a batch: softmax over similarities, then -log of the correct one.

Used for: Trains embeddings by contrast.

Real life: A matching game: match each query to its own product among 256 cards.

Where it's used: ShopRank two-tower; the same idea trains models like CLIP.

In-batch negativesWeek 3

Other rows' products in the batch, used as wrong answers.

Used for: Free negative examples, no extra labels.

Real life: In a class photo, everyone else is "not you".

Where it's used: ShopRank batch 256 = 255 negatives per query.

False negativeWeek 3

A "wrong answer" that is actually correct.

Used for: Must be masked, or the model learns the wrong lesson.

Real life: Your twin in the class photo gets marked as "not you".

Where it's used: ShopRank masks rows with the same query.

TemperatureWeek 3

Divide similarities before softmax; low = sharp, high = flat.

Used for: Controls how strong the learning signal is.

Real life: Zooming in on small differences between scores.

Where it's used: 0.05 worked; 1.0 barely learned (loss stayed near ln 256).

Hard negativesWeek 3

Wrong products that look similar to the right one.

Used for: Teach the model fine differences.

Real life: Practising to tell an iPhone from an iPhone case, not from a sofa.

Where it's used: Week 3 lacked them, one reason it lost; fix in week 5.

Pretrained modelWeek 3

A model already trained on huge text before your task.

Used for: Brings language knowledge for free.

Real life: Hiring an experienced salesman instead of training a fresher from zero.

Where it's used: Week 3 started from random numbers; week 4 uses pretrained transformers.

AdamWWeek 3

An optimiser with adaptive step sizes per weight plus weight decay.

Used for: The standard optimiser for deep learning.

Real life: A driver who adjusts speed for each road separately.

Where it's used: ShopRank: AdamW 1e-3.

Batch / epochWeek 3

Batch = examples used per update. Epoch = one full pass over the training data.

Used for: Controls training speed and stability.

Real life: Batch = one page of practice; epoch = finishing the whole book once.

Where it's used: ShopRank: batch 256, 3 epochs, 163,125 pairs.

Overfit-one-batch testWeek 3

Train on one small batch; a correct pipeline should drive its loss near 0.

Used for: A quick bug check.

Real life: If a student can't memorise one page, something is wrong with the method.

Where it's used: ShopRank fell to 0.37 because 41 rows had duplicate product text.

GPU / CUDAWeek 3

GPU = a chip with thousands of small cores. CUDA = NVIDIA's way to program it.

Used for: Makes matrix maths fast for training.

Real life: 1,000 workers doing small sums together instead of one person.

Where it's used: RTX 4060; peak 1.3 GB in week 3.
